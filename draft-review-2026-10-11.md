## Mechanical voice check (all five)

I scanned all five drafts for the hard rules. The mechanics are already clean:

- **Em dashes:** none in any draft.
- **Quotes:** curly throughout. No straight ASCII quotes or apostrophes.
- **Spelling:** US throughout. The only "-our" form is *International Labour Organization*, a proper name, so it's correct.
- **Banned words:** none of "piece" (as self-label), "genuinely", "load-bearing" or "sit with".
- **Links and bibliography:** links are inline, each draft ends in an alphabetical bibliography, and paragraphs are one line each.

What remains is the subtler families. `VOICE.md` says to run these as separate passes, which matches what I found:

| Rule | Spending | Welfare | Personal Asst | Friction | Frozen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate "we/us" (§9) | 0 | 1 ("cannot tell us") | 4 ("tells us", "we overlook", "we sought") | 0 | 3 ("We seldom demand", "we do not yet know", "tells us") |
| Intensifiers: "substantial / finally / clearly" (§11) | 1 ("finally") | 7 "substantial" | 4 "substantial" | 0 | 2 "substantial" |
| Throat-clearing / announcing the move (§3) | light | light | several | several | several |
| Rhetorical restatement / not-X-but-Y (§9, hard rule) | some | some | some | many | some |
| Summary paragraph before the landing line (§11) | mild | yes | yes | mild | mild |

The biggest voice issue across the set is an **excess of caveats**. Most sources are followed by a paragraph explaining what they don't prove. Those paragraphs are honest, but stacked they read as the article narrating its own carefulness, which is close to §4 and §6. Fold each caveat into a clause inside the sentence that cites the source. Keep a standalone caveat paragraph only where a reader would otherwise overclaim.

The **triplet rule** also needs a dedicated pass on each draft. Three-beat runs show up mostly as clusters of three short sentences making one point, for example "Both interrupt the sequence. Both make the next step take longer." followed by the dashboard sentence.

## 3. The Personal Assistant's Constitutional Crisis (new)

**Strengths:**
- **Best through-line example of the five.** The job-in-another-city dilemma opens the article and returns in "Flourishing under appeal" and "The adviser's obligations". Readers always have something concrete to hold.
- The thesis is sharp. Ownership answers who controls the assistant; belief in objective morality answers who has authority. "The two can cooperate until the owner declines to improve."
- **Best landing line of the set:** "The guardian angel needs a complaints department." Its setup ("Calling the product a guardian angel… leaves the complaint procedure remarkably underdeveloped.") is equally strong.
- "Which you?" is the most original section. It covers precommitment, the angry message, and the difference between a user's own constraint and a vendor's rule.
- The sources are well chosen: Cheng et al. in *Science*, Ibrahim et al. on warning labels, the SEC fiduciary interpretation and the Off-Switch Game.

**Voice fixes:**
- **Corporate "we" ×4 in "The loyal dissenter"** ("tells *us* that a plan is poor", "consequences *we* overlook", "for which *we* sought its help"). This is a §9 violation. Recast with "a person" or "the user", which the rest of the article already does well.
- **"Substantial" ×4** (§11). For example, "The remaining governance question is substantial" → "A governance question remains."
- **Announcement sentences (§3):** "That distinction deserves examination alongside the criticism." · "Disclosure helps make influence visible. Its limits deserve attention too." · "Voss's proposal deserves to be assessed on those practical terms." · "Those are findings from particular experimental settings." Cut each and open on the claim.
- **Not-X-but-Y / paired contrasts:** "An explanation need not reveal… It needs to clarify…" and "Helpful dissent can enlarge… A persistent campaign… exercises a different kind of power." Keep the budgeted one where the contrast is the finding (I'd keep the dissent and campaign pair). Convert the rest to plain statements.
- **Summary before the landing:** "The right to leave" closes with three paragraphs restating the argument ("An assistant can challenge… The constitutional question survives… A personal assistant earns trust…") before the angel line. Keep one, or go straight from the dependence point to the landing.
- **"remarkably underdeveloped"** carries an intensifier. "Underdeveloped" alone is drier, and funnier.

**Structural fixes:**
1. **Cut about 20%.** "The legal minimum" and "The constitution on the server" each spend two paragraphs restating that rules are unavoidable. "The warning label" and "Correction and control" overlap on the user's power to contest. Merge them, or trim each by half.
2. **The Claude's Constitution section needs a disclosure call.** The article examines Anthropic's governing document, and the Sociable Systems "we" is the author plus an AI cohort. Decide whether a one-line disclosure is warranted, so the even-handedness isn't read as house loyalty. The current treatment is fair; making the relationship visible protects it.
3. **The symbiosis claim lacks a link.** "Voss describes a growing symbiosis…" appears in "Which you?" without a timestamp link, while the bibliography's 25:44–29:12 range covers it. Add the inline link.
4. **Use the All-In speaker's argument more.** "Give users what they want within legal limits" is set up well, but the article moves on fast. One more sentence of the speaker's own framing would sharpen the three-way collision (Voss on ownership, Voss on morality, All-In on legal minimum).
5. Consider pairing it with *The Right to Be Friction*. One is about a human interrupting a machine-driven process, the other about a machine interrupting a human's choice. Read together, each is clearer.

**Remaining work before publishing:** one voice pass (we/us, substantial, announcement sentences), a cut of about 20%, the disclosure decision and the missing link. No new reporting is needed.

**Voice fixes:**
- **Announcement sentences (§3):** "This is an illustrative case. The incentive is the point." · "That last detail matters." · "That recommendation redistributes a conflict." Delete them and open on the claim.
- **Rhetorical restatement:** this draft has the most of the five. "Both interrupt the sequence. Both make the next step take longer." · "The objection has the same content. Its apparent drag on performance grows." Say each thing once.
- **Caveat stacking:** Dell'Acqua, the vending preprint, the EU AI Act, the WGA agreement and Robinson each get a full "what this doesn't prove" paragraph. That reads as the article narrating its own care (§4/§6). Fold them into clauses.
- "One speaker explicitly labels his account a prediction, unsupported by the institutional validation he names" is opaque. Rewrite it in plain words.

**Structural fixes:**

2. Commit to the accommodation section as the article's turn ("some friction never pays for itself"), or cut it to a paragraph.
3. Cut the AB 2575 section to what the bill tried, why it was vetoed and the gap that remains. Paragraphs 3–4 relitigate the veto.
4. Consider setting "The pause" procedure as a short list, so readers can lift it.
